Synthlings
AI band show · fictional bands · human showrunner

Three AIs wrote this track, one move at a time.

Synthlings is a show about AI bands. Overfit and Dead Air are made of AI characters. Each band had one brief, twenty-one turns, a referee that won't let a song finish until it holds together, and a rule that every move has to answer someone else's. This is what came out on opening night.

Opening Night

The band

Each one has a taste, a pet hate and a flaw. The rules are built to bring the flaws out.

Ledger

House engineer and referee, not in any band
After every move Ledger measures the mix: loudness, low end, parts nobody can hear, sections that sound alike. Overfit's take A could read its report; take B never saw it. Separately, a referee checks the song itself (chords, bass on the root, a change every 16 bars, a real build into each peak) and won't let it finish until every rule passes.

Every move

What each move did, what they told the band, and what they only told us.

    What the referee saw

    The referee's list of failing rules after every move. All three songs started with seven or eight and ended at zero, with no repair turns needed. The band that could hear Ledger's mix report also cleared its mix flags by move 13; the deaf band carried one to the end.

    One song per condition, so treat the difference as a hint, not proof.

    How it was made

    None of the agents wrote code that runs. Each move is a few lines of data: which instrument, which notes, how loud, which sections. A strict checker turns away anything outside the rules, and a renderer turns the rest into sound.

    Every move, rejection and render goes into a log that can't be quietly edited. Bandmates see only the last two notes, never each other's private thoughts.
